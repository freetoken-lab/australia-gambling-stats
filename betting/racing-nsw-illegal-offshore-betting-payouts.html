Racing NSW Wants Banks to Block Payouts From Illegal Offshore Bookmakers

Racing NSW's top lawyer says stopping winnings from reaching Australian bank accounts would do more to deter betting with unlicensed operators than blocking deposits alone — an idea left out of the gambling reforms passed in August.

Tom Pascoe Sports Betting Correspondent
|
Fact Checked by Liam Thorne, Senior Editor
| 📅 Published: 25 November 2026

Racing NSW general counsel Pete Sweney has urged Australia to stop illegal offshore bookmakers from paying winnings into Australian bank accounts, arguing the step would discourage punters from using unlicensed operators more effectively than stopping money from leaving the country.

What Racing NSW is proposing

Sweney's case rests on punter behaviour. If customers of an illegal operator risk not being able to collect their winnings, he argues, they will be less inclined to place bets with it in the first place. In his view, blocking incoming transfers would work better than the current focus on blocking outbound payments to illegal wagering sites.

The racing industry raised the idea of restricting incoming payments during consultation on the gambling reforms passed in August, but it was not adopted. Racing bodies are among those most exposed when wagering moves offshore, because it takes tax receipts and the contributions that help fund racing and sport with it. For the licensed side of the market, see our guide to sports betting in Australia, which covers market size, operators and state-by-state wagering taxes.

What the August reforms require instead

The legislated measures take a different route. They require banks and payment-system participants to take reasonably practicable steps to prevent transfers to illegal online gambling services — targeting money flowing out of Australian accounts rather than winnings flowing in. According to ASEAN Gaming's report on Sweney's written comments, those requirements take effect on 1 January 2027.

A call for national consistency

Sweney also wants a consistent national prohibition on betting with unlicensed operators, backed by coordinated federal action on payments and online access. He pointed to differences between state and territory laws, arguing that offshore operators can exploit the gaps between them.

Any extension of payment restrictions to money received from illegal operators, he said, should come with clear identification rules, review rights and safeguards against wrongly blocked transactions. He also urged banks, payment providers and digital platforms to agree on how quickly they would act once an illegal operator has been identified.

How big is the offshore market? A Responsible Wagering Australia report, cited by Betsy last year, estimated that the illegal offshore betting market had doubled since 2019 and that offshore operators were taking about 36% of online wagering in Australia. RWA represents licensed operators, so treat these as industry estimates rather than government figures.

What it means for punters

Nothing Sweney proposed is law, and the measures already legislated focus on outbound payments. The practical point for bettors is unchanged: illegal offshore bookmakers are not licensed in Australia, so they sit outside BetStop and Australian consumer protections. If gambling is causing you harm, free 24/7 support is available on 1800 858 858.

Background

Racing NSW is the controlling body for thoroughbred racing in New South Wales, the state that generates the largest share of Australian wagering; our NSW gambling statistics page tracks its losses, tax revenue and reform timeline. For national figures on turnover, participation and harm, see Australian gambling statistics and news on our homepage.
