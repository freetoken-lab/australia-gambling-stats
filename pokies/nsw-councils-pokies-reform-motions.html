NSW Councils Pass Pokies Reform Motions, Piling Pressure on the Minns Government

Councils from Inner West to Georges River have backed calls for cashless gambling and overnight pokie shutdowns, adding to pressure on a government that announced its own reform package in August but stopped short of mandatory cashless gaming.

Sarah Kowalski Senior Journalist — Gambling & Regulation
|
Fact Checked by Liam Thorne, Senior Editor
| 📅 Published: 25 November 2026

Councils across New South Wales have passed motions at the Local Government NSW Annual Conference in Wollongong calling for stronger protections against poker machine harm, and Wesley Mission is urging the Minns Government to act on them.

What the councils decided

Motions brought by Inner West and Georges River councils were approved unanimously, according to Wesley Mission. They add to earlier calls for stronger gambling protections from Tweed Shire, Lismore, Wagga Wagga, Newcastle and the City of Sydney.

The motions back three measures: mandatory cashless gambling with harm-prevention features built in, poker machines switched off overnight, and greater investment in prevention and support services. They are requests to the state government rather than law.

Wesley Mission's message to the government

Wesley Mission CEO Rev Stu Cameron said councils see the damage pokies do to families up close, and argued that gambling harm does not stop at council boundaries, making it a statewide problem that needs a statewide solution. He framed the decision facing the Minns Government as a choice between the communities asking for protection and an industry earning billions from poker machines.

The numbers behind the push

Wesley Mission says NSW residents lost $4.75 billion to poker machines in the first six months of 2026, which is $311 million more than in the same period a year earlier. For the longer picture, including the record $9.3 billion lost in 2025, see our guide to pokies in Australia and our NSW gambling statistics page.

A note on the figures: The councils' motions and the loss figures above come from Wesley Mission, which campaigns for pokies reform, so they reflect an advocacy group's framing. Official NSW machine and expenditure data is published by Liquor & Gaming NSW.

Where NSW reform stands

The motions follow a reform package the state government announced on 25 August. It centres on a statewide exclusion register backed by mandatory facial recognition at the entry to gaming rooms, expected from 2028. It also lifts the forfeiture rate on traded machine entitlements from one in three to one in two, bans VIP programs tied to gaming, and prohibits gambling advertising on government and council-owned property.

The package stopped short of mandatory account-based, cashless gambling, which is what the councils and Wesley Mission want. Gaming and Racing Minister David Harris has argued that facial recognition and the exclusion register matter more than cutting machine numbers, and at least one Labor mayor criticised the package as not going far enough.

Background

New South Wales has more poker machines than any other Australian state, and its losses are concentrated in a small number of western Sydney areas. For national figures on turnover, participation and harm, see Australian gambling statistics and news on our homepage.
